# Omni-Compass: five-reviewer consensus audit

Repository commit 1e0f4c3 (the delivered zip), audited 27 September 2026. The audit reran the zip from a clean copy.
`verify.py` passed. The held-out result files regenerated byte-identical. A fresh fault sample was drawn. The live
lever proof passed on real Kubernetes in GitHub Actions run 36270589830 (19 of 19 checks).

**Independence.** The five reviews below were written by the same assistant that built most of this code. They are an
internal pre-review, organised the way an external panel would organise one. They are not an independent referee
report. Every grade cites evidence a third party can rerun.

Grades: A (ready as is), B (sound, with named gaps), C (usable only with material caveats), D (not supported).

## 1. The five reviewers

### Computational auditor: correctness and reproducibility (grade A-)

**Findings**
- `verify.py` passed on a clean copy.
- The C++ core ran a 100,000,000-decision soak at about 2.3 µs per decision, with no failures and flat memory.
- Python/C++ parity checks pass.
- The closure-law held-out run (41 losing cells) and the whole-body site league reproduced exactly.
- Every tuned setting was frozen by SHA-256 before its held-out run (`tuning/*PREREGISTRATION*.json`).
- Every simulation lever defaults to off, and was checked to leave earlier results unchanged, trace hash for trace hash.

**Deductions**
- Held-out seed sets were spent round after round (700201 → 700301 → 700501 → 700601 → 700801 → 700901 → 701001). Each
  set is used once. But choosing *which rounds to report* is itself a researcher degree of freedom.
- The bootstrap loss rule uses 30 scenarios per cell. Small true differences can be missed.

### Mechanism-of-action reviewer: does the mathematics do the work? (grade A-)

**Findings**
- The six-state core is stable. Its Jacobian modes are λ = −7.9, −3.1, −0.72, −0.20 ± 0.98i and −0.17, giving time
  constants of 1.3 to 60 decisions.
- The operating point is U = 0.976, inside the healthy basin.
- Assimilation and the equations each carry about half of every state move (`tools/mechanism.py`).
- The closure law (`omnicompass/closure.py`) implements the manuscript directly:
  - forward projection (Section 5c);
  - F = G0 + Gc with no correction in the interior and an inward correction near ∂Ω, i.e. G·n ≤ 0 (Chapter 20);
  - release only after the turning point (Chapters 29-30);
  - a dual-bath level/rate tracker (Chapter 31).
- Two further pieces are physical derivations, not tuned knobs:
  - the warm-reserve horizon at the break-even BOOT_TICKS / park_frac = 24 ticks;
  - square-root and M/M/c staffing.
- Ablation shows each piece moves the gauges it should. With the closure law off, Omni-alone energy is 26% worse than
  Kubernetes. With it on, energy is 20-22% better.

**Deductions**
- The six-state core feeds the closure law only through its push term (the release gate). The machine decisions are
  made by the closure law.
- The core's own controller output is not applied during governance (by design, and stated).

### Editor: claims discipline and clarity (grade B+)

**Findings**
- The report separates **measured / simulated / emulated** results.
- Every negative is printed: 41 losing cells for C against all platforms, 3 then 0 for B, and the failed live runs.
- The engine interpretation notice is present.
- Superseded numbers are labelled.

**Deductions**
- The report has grown section by section (6, 6A, 6B, 6C). A buyer-facing edition needs one clean structure.
- The patience round against Kubernetes and OpenShift is in `tuning/` but not yet in the report.
- Several terms from the manuscript (conveyance, basin) are used without a one-line engineering gloss.

### Underwriter: what can be relied on, and what could go wrong (grade B-)

**Findings**
- Live evidence on real Kubernetes:
  - the least-privilege identity is proven with `kubectl auth can-i` receipts;
  - the kill switch restores every lever, including from a fresh process;
  - one lever failing no longer blocks the others (this bug was found live and fixed);
  - a fail-safe hands control back to native after repeated failures.
- The live run in `LIVE_ALLMUSCLE_6` (B on kind) measured 47% fewer nodes and 20-22% better p95/p99.

**Deductions and risks**
- The live evidence is kind on one CI machine, with power modelled rather than metered.
- There is no production or customer data.
- Traffic shift assumes replicated services and is simulated only.
- CPU-frequency and power caps are not proven on hardware.
- Cooling writes to a stand-in controller.
- In the public cloud, parked machines still bill. The energy saving from the warm reserve applies on owned hardware.
- The competitor emulations reproduce documented behaviour on a fluid plant. Their production binaries may differ.

### Appraiser: value and market position (grade B)

**Findings**
- B (on top of any platform) is the strongest commercial asset.
  - Held-out: 0 losing cells after the fixes, and 27 of 28 platform-workload pairs strictly better.
  - Four-cluster sites: 0 losses on all 7 platforms.
- C (Omni alone), against Kubernetes/OpenShift shops:
  - energy 20-23% better;
  - machine-hours 34-38% better;
  - slowest responses 46-60% faster;
  - fewer pages under faults (14-25%).
- C at four-cluster sites: zero losses against CAST AI, Karpenter, Spot and GKE.
- The category (the capacity and cost-optimisation layer: HPA, VPA, Cluster Autoscaler, Karpenter, KEDA, CAST AI, Spot,
  Turbonomic) is a paid, established market.

**Deductions**
- Against the tight packers on single clusters, Omni alone roughly ties on energy. The measured advantage there is
  latency and stability, not the bill.
- Price-aware features (spot instances, instance types) are absent.
- There is no customer reference yet.

**Consensus: B+ overall.**
- Engineering and mathematics: A-.

- Bankability: C+ until a customer pilot.

The single highest-value next step is a shadow-mode pilot on a real customer cluster with metered power.
